=====================================================================
On testing recovery, not just taking backups
=====================================================================

"No one really wants to make backups; what people really want are
restores."

"you only know that you can recover your recent state if you actually do
so"

"If you discover that your restore process is broken before you need to
rely upon it, you can address the vulnerability before you fall victim to
it"

"Continuously test the recovery process as part of your normal operations"

"Set up alerts that fire when a recovery process fails to provide a
heartbeat indication of its success."

"The only test that should let you sleep at night is a full end-to-end
test."

"If recovery tests are a manual, staged event, testing becomes an unwelcome
bit of drudgery that isn't performed either deeply or frequently enough to
deserve your confidence."

"automate these tests whenever possible and then run them continuously"

Relevance: Google's framing draws a hard distinction between "a backup
exists" and "recovery is proven" — and explicitly names "a full end-to-end
test" as the only one that earns confidence, contrasted with manual,
infrequent, partial tests. This is a data-point in favor of the engineer's
premise that fidelity to the real recovery path matters, though this
source is about Google's internal practice at Google's scale, not a
compliance/audit standard — it is not itself evidence that an auditor
would refuse a lower-fidelity test, only that Google's own engineering
philosophy treats untested/manual/partial restores as insufficient
assurance.
