CORE DEFINITION OF JUST CULTURE:
A "Just Culture" as an organizational approach that "balance[s] safety and accountability"
by investigating failures through the lens of situational context and decision-making
processes rather than individual blame.

WHAT BLAMELESS POSTMORTEMS ENABLE (verbatim paraphrase from mirror):
A blameless postmortem process allows engineers to provide detailed accounts
"without fear of punishment or retribution." This transparency covers:
- Actions taken and timing
- Observed effects
- Expectations held
- Assumptions made
- Understanding of event timeline

KEY QUOTE ON FEAR:
"an engineer who thinks they're going to be reprimanded are _disincentivized_
to give the details necessary."

THE SECOND STORY CONCEPT:
Rather than asking "what should have been done," effective postmortems seek understanding
of why actions "made sense to them at the time."

BLAME CYCLE:
Punishment creates a self-reinforcing cycle leading to reduced trust, silent engineers,
management ignorance, and ultimately more errors — a pattern described as
"Cover-Your-Ass engineering."

ACCOUNTABILITY NOTE:
Blameless approaches do not remove accountability. Instead, engineers become actively
responsible for improving organizational safety through their expertise in failures
they've experienced.

CULTURAL ORIGIN NOTE:
Allspaw's contribution was translating safety science from aviation and healthcare and
