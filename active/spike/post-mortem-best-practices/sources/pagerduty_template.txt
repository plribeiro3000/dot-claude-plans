=== VERBATIM SECTION EXTRACTS ===

SECTION 1 — Overview:
"Include a short sentence or two summarizing the contributing factors, timeline summary, and the impact."

SECTION 2 — What Happened:
"Include a short description of what happened."

SECTION 3 — Contributing Factors:
"Include a description of any conditions that contributed to the issue. If there were any actions taken that exacerbated the issue, also include them here."

SECTION 4 — Resolution:
"Include a description of what solved the problem. If there was a temporary fix in place, describe that along with the long-term solution."

SECTION 5 — Impact:
"Be very specific here and include exact numbers."
Tracks:
- Time in SEV-1
- Time in SEV-2
- Notifications Delivered out of SLA
- Events Dropped/Not Accepted
- Accounts Affected
- Users Affected
- Support Requests Raised

SECTION 6 — Responders:
Lists: Incident Commander, scribe, and other involved personnel.

SECTION 7 — Timeline:
"Some important times to include: (1) time the contributing factor began, (2) time of the page, (3) time that the status page was updated, (4) time of any significant actions, (5) time the SEV-2/1 ended, (6) links to tools/logs."

SECTION 8 — How'd We Do?
Two subsections: "What Went Well?" and "What Didn't Go So Well?"

SECTION 9 — Action Items:
"Each action item should be in the form of a JIRA ticket" with specific tagging conventions.

SECTION 10 — Messaging:
Internal Email and External Message subsections for communicating findings.

=== KEY PROCESS PRINCIPLES (from https://postmortems.pagerduty.com/culture/blameless/) ===

WHAT BLAMELESS MEANS:
"A blameless postmortem stays focused on how a mistake was made instead of who made it."

WHY BLAME FAILS:
"This impulse to blame and punish has the unintended effect of disincentivizing the knowledge sharing required to prevent future failure."

THE GOAL:
"The goal of the postmortem is to understand what systemic factors led to the incident and identify actions that can prevent this kind of failure from recurring."

QUESTION GUIDANCE:
"Ask 'what' and 'how' questions rather than 'who' or 'why.'"
Avoid "why" questions because they "force people to justify their actions, attributing blame."

FRAMING TECHNIQUE:
"When inquiring about a human action, abstract to an inspecific responder. Anyone could have made the same mistake."

MEETING SCHEDULING NOTE:
Schedule postmortem meetings within 5 business days.

NOTE ON BLAME-AWARE vs. BLAMELESS:
The PagerDuty documentation acknowledges that "the tendency to blame is hardwired through millions of years of evolutionary neurobiology." The realistic goal is being "blame aware" — recognizing cognitive biases when they arise — rather than claiming true blamelessness is achievable.
