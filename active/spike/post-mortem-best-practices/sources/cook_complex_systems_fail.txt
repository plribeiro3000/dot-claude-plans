=== ALL 18 PRINCIPLES (verbatim from https://how.complexsystems.fail/) ===

1. "Complex systems are intrinsically hazardous systems."
2. "Complex systems are heavily and successfully defended against failure"
3. "Catastrophe requires multiple failures – single point failures are not enough."
4. "Complex systems contain changing mixtures of failures latent within them."
5. "Complex systems run in degraded mode."
6. "Catastrophe is always just around the corner."
7. "Post-accident attribution to a 'root cause' is fundamentally wrong."
8. "Hindsight biases post-accident assessments of human performance."
9. "Human operators have dual roles: as producers & as defenders against failure."
10. "All practitioner actions are gambles."
11. "Actions at the sharp end resolve all ambiguity."
12. "Human practitioners are the adaptable element of complex systems."
13. "Human expertise in complex systems is constantly changing"
14. "Change introduces new forms of failure."
15. "Views of 'cause' limit the effectiveness of defenses against future events."
16. "Safety is a characteristic of systems and not of their components"
17. "People continuously create safety."
18. "Failure free operations require experience with failure."

=== KEY EXTENDED EXPLANATIONS (from https://how.complexsystems.fail/) ===

ON PRINCIPLE 7 (post-accident attribution):
"Because overt failure requires multiple faults, there is no isolated 'cause' of an accident."
Cook argues the search for single causes reflects "social, cultural need to blame specific,
localized forces" rather than technical understanding.

ON PRINCIPLE 8 (hindsight bias):
"Hindsight bias remains the primary obstacle to accident investigation, especially when
expert human performance is involved."

ON PRINCIPLE 10 (practitioner gambles):
"all practitioner actions are actually gambles, that is, acts that take place in the face
of uncertain outcomes."
Post-accident analysis misinterprets successful gambles as poor decisions due to hindsight bias.

ON PRINCIPLE 15 (views of cause):
"Post-accident remedies for 'human error'...usually increase the coupling and complexity
of the system."

ON PRINCIPLE 17 (people create safety):
Safety emerges through practitioner adaptations to changing conditions during normal operations.

=== SIGNIFICANCE FOR POST-MORTEMS ===
Cook's framework directly challenges the standard "root cause analysis" approach by arguing
that single root causes are a social construction, not a technical reality. This grounds
the theoretical case for blameless post-mortems: if failures are multi-causal and latent,
blame assigned to any one person is necessarily reductive and counterproductive.

The paper is brief (~6 pages), freely accessible, and cited as foundational reading in the
Learning From Incidents community and by Allspaw, Dekker, and Woods.
