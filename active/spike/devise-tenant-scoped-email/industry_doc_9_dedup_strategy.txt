TITLE: GitLab — Deduplicate database records (adding unique constraints to tables with
existing duplicates)

VERBATIM QUOTES:

"Duplications are rare and mostly happen due to concurrent record creation."
(Note: GitLab says duplicates are RARE. In 4Shark's case, there are 8 known collisions
on email — deliberately created, not accidental.)

"This is a destructive operation with no possibility of rolling back. Make sure that
the deduplication logic is tested thoroughly."

## Three-Milestone Strategy for Adding a Global Unique Constraint

### Milestone 1: Prevention Infrastructure
1. Add a non-unique index via post-migration
2. Implement model-level validation to reduce duplicate likelihood
3. Add an advisory lock to prevent concurrent duplicate creation

NOTE: "The second step on its own will not prevent duplicate records" because transactions
cannot see each other's uncommitted changes. The advisory lock addresses this by
blocking concurrent inserts of identical data.

### Milestone 2: Deduplication & Constraint Addition
For small tables (under 10 million rows):
- Batch scanning for duplicate key combinations
- Applying resolution logic (e.g., keeping the most recent record)
- Replacing the non-unique index with a unique one

For large tables: move this logic into a batched background migration.

### Milestone 3: Cleanup & Enforcement
Remove the advisory lock callback after a required deployment stop, finalizing
the unique constraint enforcement.

## Key Caveats for Option A (Global Unique)

- Requires ALL modifications to flow through ActiveRecord (callback-dependent)
- Offers NO ROLLBACK capability after deduplication runs
- Resolution strategy must match business logic for the specific data
- In 4Shark's case: the 8 collisions are DIFFERENT PEOPLE with the same email —
  there is no "merge" possible. Each row is a real human employee. Resolution would
  require email address CHANGES for some of the 8 users.
