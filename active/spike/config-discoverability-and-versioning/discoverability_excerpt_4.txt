SOURCE: https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04596v1
TITLE: "Need Help? Designing Proactive AI Assistants for Programming" (CHI 2025)
PURPOSE: Empirical data on when proactive suggestions help vs. harm

KEY QUOTES (verbatim from fetched page):

1. Timing mechanism:
"Suggestions are not requested when the user is interacting with a previous
suggestion, typing in the chat, sending a message, or waiting for a response."

"When the user is idle—likely due to planning and brainstorming, the assistant
provides suggestions every 5 seconds, limited to every 20 seconds since the
last suggestion."

2. Productivity gains with caveats:
"the proactive assistant improved productivity by 12-18% across conditions"

3. The overload problem:
"when a proactive assistant provides too many suggestions, participants can be
easily distracted and thus still want to view the suggestions at a similar
frequency. However, when the suggestions are provided too often, participants
are less likely to make use of the suggestions."

4. Frequency preference:
"Only 45% of participants preferred this more aggressive approach [5-second
interval], compared to 80-90% preference for the moderated versions."
"the non-proactive chat assistant was best because it didn't interrupt what I was doing."

RELEVANCE TO 4SHARK:
The research shows proactive tips improve outcomes only when:
- Rate-limited (not on every interaction)
- Surfaced during idle/planning moments (not mid-task)
- High precision (tip is actually relevant to the user's current context)

In Claude Code: "idle/planning moments" maps to session start (SessionStart hook)
and the beginning of a new task thread. "Mid-task" maps to UserPromptSubmit on
every turn. The existing inject-output-policy-reminder.sh fires on EVERY prompt —
this is the aggressive pattern (equivalent to 5-second interval in the study).

SOURCE: NN/g — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/pop-up-adaptive-help/
PURPOSE: UX guidance on adaptive help systems

KEY QUOTES (verbatim from fetched page):
"Systems cannot predict everything, and users know it."
"For most companies, the cost of developing an intelligent adaptive help system
far outweighs the benefits to the user."
"help should be 'Available without interfering' and remain 'out of the way until
users need it'"

RELEVANCE: The NN/g guidance points toward passive availability (slash commands,
CHANGELOG entries the engineer can read) over active interruption (injected tips).
The cost observation is about development cost, not token cost — but the principle
translates: an imprecise tip system is worse than no tip system.

SOURCE: https://www.command.ai/blog/what-clippy-taught-us-all/
PURPOSE: Documents Clippy failure mode

KEY QUOTES (from search result summary — verbatim):
"Clippy's number-one problem was overeagerness—imagine a waiter interrupting your
dinner conversation every 30 seconds asking if you're ready for dessert."
"Clippy lacked real contextual awareness and would offer writing tips or formatting
help based on shallow triggers, not actual user intent."

RELEVANCE: The "shallow trigger" failure mode maps directly to keyword-based hook
injection in 4Shark. A hook that fires on every UserPromptSubmit with "did you know
we have /pr-triage?" regardless of context is the Clippy anti-pattern.
