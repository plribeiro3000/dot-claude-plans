Source: https://www.aosfatos.org/noticias/anpd-chatgpt-vazamento-dados-pessoais/
===========================================================================

What is being investigated:
"violou o artigo 48 da LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados), que obriga controladoras de sistemas de coleta e armazenamento de dados a comunicar 'a ocorrência de incidente de segurança que possa acarretar risco ou dano relevante aos titulares'"

Investigation subject: whether OpenAI failed to notify users about a June 2023 data breach affecting approximately 100,000 Brazilian ChatGPT users. This is a security incident notification issue, not an API inference or training data issue.

Outcome:
"não é certo se resultará em sanção, como multa, conforme a lei prevê em caso de descumprimento"
(Translation: "it is not certain whether it will result in a sanction, such as a fine, as provided by law in the event of non-compliance")

Investigation remains ongoing. No sanction issued.

SECTION D — ANPD preliminary study on generative AI: inference addressed
Source: https://fpf.org/blog/brazils-anpd-preliminary-study-on-generative-ai-highlights-the-dual-nature-of-data-protection-law-balancing-rights-with-technological-innovation/
Published: November 29, 2024 (ANPD Technology and Research Unit)
===========================================================================

On inference-time data processing:
"Depending on the prompt, context, and information provided by the user, these interactions may generate outputs containing personal data about the user or other individuals."

On user/company obligations when using generative AI:
"Users sharing the personal data of other individuals through prompts may be considered processing agents under the LGPD and consequently be subject to its obligations and sanctioning regime."

But:
"transferring responsibility exclusively to users is not enough to safeguard personal data protection or privacy in the context of generative AI."

On lawful processing basis for inference:
"Scraping operations that capture personal data must be based on one of the LGPD's lawful bases for processing (Articles 7 and 11) and comply with data protection principles of good faith, purpose limitation, adequacy, and necessity."

NOTE: The study addresses inference-time scenarios but does not clearly separate training-time from inference-time obligations. It uses unified LGPD principles across "multiple stages in the life cycle of generative AI systems, from development to refinement of models." It does not name specific LLM API providers or address B2B inference API compliance as a distinct category.

SECTION F — Court of Rome annulment: jurisdictional grounds, not substantive
Sources:
- https://barlamantoday.com/2026/03/19/italian-court-reverses-regulators-e15-million-fine-against-openai-over-chatgpt-privacy-breach/
- https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/openai-prevails-in-landmark-italian-ai-and-gdpr-enforcement-case.html
Search result summary (March 18, 2026 ruling)
===========================================================================

The Court of Rome annulment grounds (from search result summary):
"the annulment rests entirely on a finding that the Italian Garante lacked jurisdiction after the Irish Data Protection Commission became OpenAI's lead supervisory authority on February 15, 2024 - months before the Garante issued its final decision."

What the Court DID NOT rule on:
"The court's reasoning does not reach the substantive GDPR violations alleged by the Italian authority. It does not assess whether OpenAI failed to notify the Garante of the March 20, 2023 data breach. It does not determine whether ChatGPT's training data processing lacked a valid legal basis under Articles 5 and 6."

Original fine: €15 million (November 2, 2024, Garante Decision No. 755)
Annulment date: March 18, 2026, Court of Rome
Full reasoning published: May 28, 2026

The annulment was jurisdictional (one-stop-shop mechanism), not a finding that the conduct was lawful. The substantive GDPR question (whether training data processing was legal) remains unanswered by any court.

SECTION G — LGPD Art. 33 mechanisms: contractual clauses as independent basis
Source: https://www.mayerbrown.com/pt/insights/publications/2024/08/new-anpd-regulation-international-data-transfers
Source: https://legale.com.br/blog/lgpd-transferencia-internacional-de-dados-e-compliance/
===========================================================================

Available mechanisms under CD/ANPD No. 19/2024:
"decisão de adequação, cláusulas contratuais específicas, cláusulas-padrão contratuais e normas corporativas globais"
(Translation: "adequacy decision, specific contractual clauses, standard contractual clauses, and global corporate rules")

Standard contractual clauses implementation requirement:
"As cláusulas-padrão contratuais se encontram anexa a essa atualização. Elas devem ser adotadas em sua integralidade, sem qualquer modificação"
(Translation: "The standard contractual clauses are attached to this update. They must be adopted in their entirety, without any modification.")

Deadline for existing contracts:
"12 (doze) meses para que os agentes de tratamento (controladores ou operadores) alterem os contratos vigentes"
(Translation: "12 [twelve] months for processing agents [controllers or operators] to amend existing contracts")
— deadline was August 23, 2025.

Processors must also comply:
"cabe tanto ao controlador como ao operador comprovar o cumprimento do Regulamento"
(Translation: "it is the responsibility of both the controller and the processor to demonstrate compliance with the Regulation")
"cabe a ele também documentar a transferência e a sua conformidade com a LGPD"
(Translation: "[the processor] must also document the transfer and its compliance with the LGPD")

Transfer-as-extension-of-jurisdiction principle:
"Elas funcionam como uma extensão da jurisdição brasileira em território estrangeiro por via obrigacional"
(Translation: "They function as an extension of Brazilian jurisdiction in foreign territory through contractual obligation")

Liability note:
"a elaboração e revisão desses instrumentos contratuais exigem precisão técnica cirúrgica, pois a responsabilidade em caso de incidente pode recair solidariamente"
(Translation: "the drafting and review of these contractual instruments require surgical technical precision, since liability in case of an incident may be shared")

No US adequacy decision:
"não houve reconhecimento formal de qualquer país como adequado."
(Translation: "no formal recognition of any country as adequate [has been issued].")
— The US has no adequacy decision from ANPD. Contractual clauses (Art. 33 Inciso II) are therefore the operative mechanism for US-based processors.

SECTION H — Cloud = international transfer: LGPD confirmed
Source: https://legale.com.br/blog/lgpd-transferencia-internacional-de-dados-e-compliance/
===========================================================================

Cloud hosting abroad constitutes international transfer:
"O armazenamento de dados em nuvem (cloud computing) com servidores no exterior é considerado transferência internacional"
(Translation: "The storage of data in the cloud [cloud computing] with servers abroad is considered an international transfer.")

This means: 4Shark using AWS us-east-1 for the `app` backend already triggers LGPD Art. 33 requirements. Adding an LLM API provider (e.g., Anthropic with DPA + SCCs) is the SAME legal category — both are international transfers to US-based processors. The operative question is whether the DPA + contractual clause mechanism (Art. 33 Inciso II) is in place, not whether the provider is "cloud" or "AI."

NOTE: The legale.com.br article does not specifically address AI API providers or LLM vendors as operadores. The cloud-parity inference is drawn from the stated principle (cloud storage abroad = international transfer) applied to the LLM API case.

Key additional finding from this source:
Resolution CD/ANPD No. 19/2024 established "greater legal certainty to processing agents and interpretive uniformity to Article 33 of the LGPD."

Art. 33 Inciso II is an INDEPENDENT mechanism from adequacy — it does not require a country adequacy decision. The fact that the US has no ANPD adequacy decision does not block transfers to US-based processors if contractual clauses are in place. This is confirmed by the Mayer Brown analysis of CD/ANPD No. 19/2024.
