# Auxiliary Source 1 — Amazon "Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit" (Primary Sources)

## Source A: Wikipedia — Disagree and commit
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disagree_and_commit
Fetched: 2026-06-23

### Full principal text extracted

Disagree and commit is a management principle that states that "individuals are allowed to disagree while a decision is being made, but that once a decision has been made, everybody must commit to implementing the decision."

**Origins — Scott McNealy (Sun Microsystems, 1983–1991):**
McNealy articulated the phrase as: "Agree and commit, disagree and commit, or get out of the way."

**Origins — Andy Grove (Intel):**
Grove demanded that team members "argue well" in meetings yet leave "fully committed to the decisions that had been made."

**Amazon's formal adoption (~2010–2011):**
Official principle text: "Leaders are obligated to respectfully challenge decisions when they disagree, even when doing so is uncomfortable or exhausting. Leaders have conviction and are tenacious. They do not compromise for the sake of social cohesion. Once a decision is determined, they commit wholly."

## Source B: Jeff Bezos 2016 Letter to Amazon Shareholders
URL: https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2016-letter-to-shareholders
Fetched: 2026-06-23

### Verbatim passages

**On the phrase itself:**
"use the phrase 'disagree and commit.' This phrase will save a lot of time."

**On the mechanism — inviting commitment after disagreement:**
"If you have conviction on a particular direction even though there's no consensus, it's helpful to say, 'Look, I know we disagree on this but will you gamble with me on it? Disagree and commit?'"

**On bidirectionality (boss also commits to subordinate's direction):**
"This isn't one way. If you're the boss, you should do this too. I disagree and commit all the time. We recently greenlit a particular Amazon Studios original. I told the team my view: debatable whether it would be interesting enough, complicated to produce, the business terms aren't that good, and we have lots of other opportunities. I wrote back right away with 'I disagree and commit and hope it becomes the most watched thing we've ever made.'"

**On what the phrase represents:**
"a genuine disagreement of opinion, a candid expression of my view, a chance for the team to weigh my view, and a quick, sincere commitment to go their way."

**On two-way vs one-way decisions (Type 2 / Type 1 distinction):**
"Many decisions are reversible, two-way doors. Those decisions can use a light-weight process. For those, so what if you're wrong? [Irreversible decisions] must be made methodically, carefully, slowly, with great deliberation and consultation."

## Source C: Leading Sapiens deep dive
URL: https://www.leadingsapiens.com/amazon-leadership-principles-deep-dive/

### Commit trigger mechanism

"Leaders must actively voice dissent and argue their position, regardless of rank or comfort level."

"After a decision is made, leaders shift entirely to full support."

"The transition occurs when 'a decision is determined.' The article emphasizes this is not compromise—it's complete commitment to the chosen direction once leadership settles on it. The brevity of the commit phase ('quick, sincere commitment') suggests the decision point itself triggers the shift."

## Source D: The Hard Thing About Disagree and Commit (Management for Startups)
URL: https://managementforstartups.com/articles/the-hard-thing-about-disagree-and-commit/
Fetched: 2026-06-23

### Critique — the difference between compliance and real commitment

"he complied with his boss's directions while simultaneously 'grouched about [his] disagreements…to [his] entire team,' inadvertently painting leadership as unreasonable. This created misalignment without authentic commitment."

"Real commitment means executing the decision as if you supported it, rather than performing compliance while undermining it."

### When disagree-and-commit breaks down (three scenarios identified)

1. Loss of trust in leadership
2. Team resistance stronger than your own disagreement
3. Emotional exhaustion from constant strategic shifts

"staying in your company when you feel this way is a disservice to yourself, to your company, and to the subordinates."

## Source E: Andy Grove / Intel origin
URL: https://slab.com/blog/andy-grove-startup-culture/
Fetched: 2026-06-23 (via search result)

### Grove's original formulation

"'disagree and commit' is the predecessor of 'alignment'. This required people to express their point of view before a decision was made, but then align to whatever decision was made even if it wasn't their first choice."

"He once told a VP, 'if you disagree with me and don't argue, you're worthless to me.' 'Debates are like the process through which a photographer sharpens the contrast when developing a print,' Grove writes."
